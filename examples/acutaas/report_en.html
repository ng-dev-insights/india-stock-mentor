The business in brief

Acutaas (called Ami Organics until 2025, based in Surat) makes the chemical building blocks that drug companies turn into medicines (pharma intermediates). Think of a company making engine parts for car makers: it doesn’t sell cars, but cars can’t be built without it. It is also growing a newer business in specialty chemicals for batteries and semiconductors.

What’s going well

  • Fast growth: in FY26, sales rose 33% and profit rose 124%.
  • Wider margins: operating margin rose from about 23% to 36%.
  • Almost no debt, with net cash in hand.
  • Management raised FY27 guidance to about 30% revenue growth (from 25%) and a 38–40% EBITDA margin.

What to watch out for

  • Expensive: P/E of about 89, roughly double the broad sector average (~46), though close contract-manufacturing peers also trade at 80–95. The stock is up about 135% in a year, so high expectations are already in the price.
  • The newer specialty chemicals business (battery and semiconductor chemicals) shrank about 20% in the latest quarter. The diversification story isn’t proven yet.
  • Very volatile: it moves about 3.4× as much as the Nifty, and fell 10% in a single day in April 2026.
  • Promoter stake fell from about 41% after the IPO to 32.7%, mostly through new share issues (dilution) plus some promoter sales in 2025. It has been unchanged for the last 5 quarters.

The key question

Today investors pay about ₹73 for every ₹1 of yearly profit.

To earn about 12% a year (roughly what a Nifty index fund has averaged over long periods), profit must grow every year for 5 years by about 26% if investors then pay ₹40 per ₹1 of profit, or about 34% if investors then pay ₹30 per ₹1 of profit.

Growth rates and P/Es are illustrative assumptions, not forecasts.

Questions a smart investor asks next

  1. Will the company keep raising money by issuing new shares? Each issue means existing shareholders own a little less.
  2. Is the margin jump permanent (better product mix) or temporary (a few large orders)?
  3. Can it grow 25%+ a year for many years? Paying ₹73–89 for every ₹1 of profit assumes roughly that.

Today’s lesson: What does paying ₹73 for ₹1 of profit mean?

Suppose a shop earns ₹1 lakh a year and you pay ₹73 lakh for it. Even if its profit grows 25% every year, in 5 years it will earn about ₹3 lakh a year: three times as much.

If buyers then pay only ₹30 for every ₹1 of profit (still above the sector average), the shop is worth about ₹92 lakh. Profit tripled, but your money grew only about 26%, roughly 4.7% a year.

That’s why a great company and a great investment are not the same thing. The price you pay matters.
