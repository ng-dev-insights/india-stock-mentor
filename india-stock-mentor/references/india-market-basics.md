## 1. How the system fits together
- **SEBI** regulates the market. **NSE** and **BSE** are the exchanges. **NSDL** and **CDSL** are depositories that hold shares electronically. **Brokers** are the members you trade through.
- You need a **demat account** (holds shares) and a **trading account** (places orders), opened with a SEBI-registered broker after KYC (PAN, Aadhaar, bank account).
- Equity trades settle on **T+1** (next working day).
- Market hours: normal session 9:15 am – 3:30 pm IST on trading days.
- Listed companies must have at least 25% public shareholding, so promoter holding is capped at 75% (with transition rules for some cases).

## 2. Costs of investing
Every buy and sell carries costs: brokerage (zero on delivery at some brokers), Securities Transaction Tax (STT), exchange transaction charges, SEBI fee, stamp duty, GST on brokerage and charges, and DP charges when you sell from demat. For long-term delivery investing these are small. For frequent trading they add up fast: SEBI's intraday study found loss-makers spent an additional 57% of their trading losses on trading costs (FY23).
Mutual funds charge an **expense ratio** annually; direct plans are cheaper than regular plans.

## 3. Taxes on shares and equity mutual funds (as of Nov 2026)
For listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds, sold through the exchange with STT paid, for transfers on or after 23 July 2024:
- **Short-term capital gains (held 12 months or less):** 20%.
- **Long-term capital gains (held more than 12 months):** 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year; no indexation.
- **Dividends:** added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.
- **Intraday and F&O income:** treated as business income, taxed at slab rate, with different ITR forms and possibly audit requirements.
- The Income-tax Act, 2025 has replaced the 1961 Act, so section numbers have changed (old 112A for LTCG corresponds to section 198 in the new Act). Rates were unchanged when this file was written, but there have been reports of the government considering changes to LTCG tax ahead of the next Budget.
Always tell the learner: check incometax.gov.in or a CA for the current year before acting on tax.

## 4. What the regulator's data says about trading
Use these when a learner is drawn to quick trading. Present them as facts from SEBI, not as your opinion, and stay kind: the point is to protect them, not shame them.

**Futures & Options (SEBI study, Sept 2024, FY22–FY24):**
- 93% of individual F&O traders made losses over the three years; combined net losses were over ₹1.8 lakh crore.
- In FY24 alone, 91.1% of individual F&O traders (about 73 lakh people) lost money, on average about ₹1.2 lakh each.
- In options specifically, 91.5% lost money in FY24 (about 60% in futures).
- Over 75% of loss-making traders kept trading despite consecutive years of losses.
- Meanwhile proprietary traders and foreign investors as groups made profits: retail losses are someone else's gains.

**Intraday in the cash market (SEBI study, July 2024, FY23):**
- 71% (7 out of 10) of individual intraday traders made net losses.
- Among those under 30, 76% lost; among very frequent traders (500+ trades a year), 80% lost.
- Even after 3 years of experience, 54% were still loss-makers.

Summary for the learner: long-term investing in good businesses or index funds is a positive-sum activity over time (the economy and companies grow). Short-term trading, especially in F&O, is close to zero-sum before costs and negative-sum after costs, and the other side is usually professional.

Source: SEBI press releases and studies on sebi.gov.in (Sept 23, 2024 F&O study; July 24, 2024 intraday study). Newer studies may exist; search for them when a learner asks.

## 5. Scams and red flags
Teach these patterns. A beginner who can recognise them is safer than one who knows ten ratios.
- **"Guaranteed" or "fixed" monthly returns from shares.** No legitimate stock investment guarantees returns.
- **Tips groups on Telegram/WhatsApp/Instagram** offering "multibagger" or "operator" stocks, often small or penny stocks. A common pump-and-dump pattern: scamsters buy cheaply, hype the stock, and sell to followers who rush in.
- **Fake trading apps or "institutional account / IPO allotment" groups** asking you to deposit money in a bank account or UPI ID rather than through a SEBI-registered broker.
- **Anyone asking for your login, password, OTP or demat access** to "trade for you". Never share these.
- **Unregistered advisers and finfluencers** selling paid calls or courses with profit screenshots. Anyone giving buy/sell advice for a fee must be SEBI-registered (Investment Adviser or Research Analyst).
- **Pressure and urgency**: "only today", "last few slots".
- **Unsolicited "recovery" offers** after a loss, promising to get money back for a fee: often a second scam.

## 6. Where to verify and complain
- Check if a broker or adviser is registered: SEBI intermediary search on sebi.gov.in.
- Investor education: investor.sebi.gov.in, SEBI Saa₹thi app, NSE/BSE investor sections.
- Complaints against registered entities: SEBI SCORES (scores.sebi.gov.in).
- Cyber fraud: national helpline 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.
