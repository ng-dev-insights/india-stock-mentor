# Learning Path (Mode C)

Give the learner only the next 1–2 steps from their current stage. A whole syllabus at once overwhelms beginners and they quit. Find their stage from what they say, or ask one short question ("Do you already have a demat account and invest in any mutual fund?").

## Stage 0 — Foundations (before investing a rupee in shares)
Check these gently. If any is missing, this is the most valuable thing you can teach, more valuable than any stock.
- Emergency fund of ~6 months' expenses in a safe place.
- Health insurance for the family, and term life insurance if anyone depends on their income.
- No expensive debt (credit-card dues, personal loans at high interest). Paying off a 30%+ credit-card balance is a guaranteed "return" no investment can match.
- A clear goal and time horizon for the money.
Concepts: inflation, compounding, emergency fund, risk vs return.

## Stage 1 — How the market works (week 1–2)
- What a share is; NSE, BSE, SEBI; demat and trading accounts.
- How to open a demat account with a SEBI-registered broker (KYC with PAN, Aadhaar, bank account).
- Market vs limit orders, T+1 settlement, what Nifty and Sensex are.
Exercise: look up the Nifty 50 list on nseindia.com and name 5 companies whose products they use.

## Stage 2 — Start safely with funds (month 1–3)
- Mutual funds, index funds, SIP, NAV, expense ratio, direct vs regular plans.
- Why a low-cost Nifty 50 index fund SIP is a sensible first step for most beginners: diversified, cheap, no stock-picking skill needed.
- Experience a market fall while invested, and learn not to panic-stop the SIP.
Exercise: compare the expense ratios of two Nifty 50 index funds on the AMC websites or a fund platform.
(The mentor explains how to compare; the learner chooses. No specific fund recommendations.)

## Stage 3 — Understand companies (month 3–6)
- Revenue, profit, EPS, P/E, ROE, debt, cash flow, promoter holding.
- Use Mode A on companies whose products the learner knows.
- Read one annual report's Chairman's letter and Management Discussion & Analysis section.
Exercise: pick a company you use, find its 5-year revenue and profit on Screener.in, and explain in 3 lines whether it is growing.

## Stage 4 — First stocks, small and deliberate (month 6+)
- Keep the core in index/mutual funds; direct stocks as a smaller part, only in businesses they understand.
- Position size: no single stock too large a share of the portfolio.
- Write down why they bought each stock, and what would make them sell. Review every quarter.
- Taxes on gains and dividends (see market basics; confirm current rules).

## Stage 5 — Going deeper (ongoing)
- Full annual-report reading, sector-specific metrics (banks, cyclicals), valuation methods.
- Understanding (not necessarily doing) F&O, with the SEBI loss data.
- Behavioural discipline: journaling decisions and reviewing mistakes.

## Free, credible learning sources to point people to
- **Zerodha Varsity** (zerodha.com/varsity) — free and structured; check the site for which modules are available in Indian languages.
- **SEBI investor education** (investor.sebi.gov.in) and the **SEBI Saa₹thi** app — official basics.
- **NSE / BSE investor education pages** and the **NISM** (nism.ac.in) resources.
- Company annual reports themselves.
Mention that these are starting points and that the learner should check that any person or channel giving advice is SEBI-registered.
