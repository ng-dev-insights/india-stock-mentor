# Events and Signals: "What changed recently?"

Use this for the "What changed recently?" step in Mode A, or when a learner asks "any news on X?", "how were the results?", "are FIIs buying?".

The purpose is to teach the learner to read events calmly, not to generate trade triggers. Every event gets: date, source, tag (🟢 good for the business / 🔴 a concern / ⚪ neutral or mixed), importance (high / medium / low), one line on why it matters, and one line on what NOT to conclude.

## 1. How to search
- **Window:** the last ~90 days by default (roughly one quarter). If the latest quarterly result is older than 90 days, include it anyway and say how old it is.
- **Sources, in order of trust:**
  1. NSE/BSE corporate announcements and filings (results, shareholding pattern, SAST and insider-trading disclosures, board outcomes). This is the official record.
  2. The company's own press release and investor presentation.
  3. Credit rating agency releases (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings).
  4. Established business news (Business Standard, Economic Times, Mint, Moneycontrol, etc.) for context.
  5. Never: social media, Telegram/WhatsApp groups, "target price" articles, paid-tip sites.
- **Suggested searches** (adapt the company name and current quarter):
  - "[company] Q[n] FY[yy] results" (latest quarter)
  - "[company] shareholding pattern [latest quarter month year]"
  - "[company] block deal" / "[company] promoter stake" / "[company] SAST"
  - "[company] credit rating" / "[company] auditor"
  - "[company] SEBI order" / "[company] USFDA" (pharma) / "[company] order win"
- **Cross-check numbers** (results, holding %) across at least two sources, as in Mode A. Aggregator pages often misalign labels on shareholding tables.
- If nothing material happened, say so. "No major events in the last 90 days" is useful, honest information.

## 2. Event catalogue (default importance)

| Event | Default tag | Importance | Why it matters | What NOT to conclude |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quarterly results vs same quarter last year | 🟢/🔴 by direction | High | Real business performance | One quarter is not a trend; compare year-on-year, not just with the previous quarter (seasonality) |
| Guidance raised or cut | 🟢/🔴 | Medium | Management's own expectations | Guidance is a promise, not a result |
| Segment-level weakness inside strong results | 🔴 | Medium | Shows which parts of the business are struggling | One weak segment doesn't break the whole company |
| Auditor resignation or qualified audit opinion | 🔴 | High | Can signal accounting problems | Read the stated reason; sometimes it is routine rotation |
| SEBI / regulator order against company or promoters | 🔴 | High | Governance and legal risk | Check whether it is final or under appeal |
| Credit rating upgrade / downgrade | 🟢/🔴 | Medium–High | Lenders' independent view of safety | Ratings lag; they confirm more than predict |
| Promoter buying from the market | 🟢 | Medium | Owners putting in their own money | Small purchases can be symbolic |
| Promoter selling (SAST disclosure) | 🔴 or ⚪ | Medium | Owners reducing exposure | Can be for taxes, family, diversification; check size and reason |
| Promoter holding falling through new share issues (QIP, preferential) | ⚪ | Low–Medium | Dilution: each share owns a bit less | Not the same as promoters selling |
| Promoter pledge created or increased | 🔴 | High if large | Forced-selling risk in a fall | Small pledges for business loans can be benign |
| FII / FPI holding change | ⚪ | Low | Shows what big foreign funds are doing | Big funds are often wrong and reverse quickly; weak signal on its own |
| Mutual fund holding change | ⚪ | Low | Domestic professional interest | Same as above; also driven by fund inflows, not just conviction |
| Bulk / block deals | ⚪ | Low | A large holder entering or exiting | Often just a change of hands at an agreed price |
| Large order win / contract | 🟢 | Low–Medium | Possible future revenue | An announcement is not revenue yet; check size vs annual sales |
| Capex / new plant / acquisition | ⚪ | Medium | Future capacity, plus execution risk | Spending now, benefits later (maybe) |
| Plant shutdown, fire, accident | 🔴 | Medium–High | Production loss | Check insurance and how long the shutdown lasts |
| Senior management exit (CEO, CFO) | 🔴 | Medium | Possible instability | One exit is common; several in a short time is the warning |
| Dividend, bonus, split | ⚪ | Low | Cash return or share-count change | Bonus and split don't create value |

## 3. Rules for writing each event
- **Mark `kind` and `confidence` for every event.** `kind`: business (something changed in operations, results, ownership or risk) or market (a price move or fund flow). `confidence`: filing (official exchange filing or results), company (management statement, presentation, earnings call), news (media report not confirmed by the company). Never let a market reaction stand in for business performance, and treat news-only items as unconfirmed.
- **Fact first, then meaning.** "Revenue rose 43% vs the same quarter last year (company results, 27 Oct)." Then the plain-language "why it matters".
- **Always include "what NOT to conclude".** This is the line that teaches.
- **Never turn an event into an action.** No "FIIs are buying, so buy", no "results were strong, the stock will rise". You may say what kind of question the event should prompt.
- **Don't relay analyst ratings or target prices** found in news articles about the event.
- **Revisit earlier conclusions.** If a recent filing contradicts something said earlier in the conversation or in an older report (as with promoter holding being stable, not falling), correct it openly. Updating your view when facts change is a lesson in itself.
- Keep to the 4–8 most important events. A long list hides the signal.

## 4. Sector-specific events
- **Pharma / API / CDMO:** USFDA inspection outcomes (Form 483 observations, warning letters, import alerts), EIR (clean inspection), large customer contracts, patent expiries of key customer drugs.
- **Banks / NBFCs:** asset-quality changes (gross/net NPA), RBI actions, capital raises, CASA trend, large one-time provisions.
- **Cyclicals (metals, cement, chemicals):** commodity price moves, anti-dumping duties, capacity additions by competitors.
- **IT services:** large deal wins, client concentration, attrition, currency.
- **PSUs:** government disinvestment (OFS), policy changes, dividend policy.

## 5. Price moves are not events
A share jumping 14% on results day tells you what the market felt that day, not what the business is worth. Mention a big price reaction only to teach this point: when the price rises faster than profit, expectations (and the P/E) rise, which makes the next quarter's bar higher.
